# ADR-0027: M1.39 Owned Dynamic Buffer Contract

Status: Accepted for the architecture/specification campaign. It authorizes
future implementation work only after this ADR, `spec/dynamic-buffers.md`, and
`docs/milestone-1.39.md` are reviewed together. It does not implement M1.39.

## Context

The previous autonomous campaign correctly stopped because the research
roadmap named dynamic buffers/text without defining ownership, allocation,
lifetime, aliasing, source syntax, failure transport, IR, or native ABI. The
current repository's M1.33-M1.35 documents do not contain the `OwnedBuffer`,
`DynamicBytes`, `DynamicText`, or `Allocator` source contracts assumed by the
previous prompt. They explicitly exclude heap storage, dynamic strings, and
heap objects. M1.39 must therefore define the first canonical source model.

The existing S3 architecture favors fixed layouts, explicit mutability,
deterministic bounds, conservative alias analysis, a private native address
representation, and no source pointer values. The decision must extend those
properties without copying a complete Rust, Zig, or C++ memory model.

## Decision

M1.39 introduces two ordinary, move-only, owned source values: `bytes` and
`text`. A `bytes` value owns arbitrary octets. A `text` value owns valid UTF-8
bytes. They are not generic types and do not expose handles or pointers.

Borrowed access uses the existing `&bytes`, `&mut bytes`, `&text`, and
`&mut text` forms. Borrows are lexical, non-owning, and checked for owner
validity. Multiple shared borrows are allowed; mutable access excludes all
other borrows of that owner. The backend must not infer machine `noalias` from
the source rule.

Allocation is explicit at constructors, clone, concat, and `reserve`. Capacity
is exact and growth is not implicit. The logical maximum is 2,147,483,647
bytes and the default active limit is 67,108,864 bytes. Allocation failure,
capacity failure, bounds failure, and invalid UTF-8 are deterministic runtime
traps in M1.39, not a new generic Result system.

Destruction is compiler-inserted at lexical scope exits. A move transfers one
drop obligation. No GC, reference counting, source `free`, or public raw
pointer is introduced.

Within S3, owned descriptors transfer in logical `(base, length, capacity)`
order. At C boundaries only borrowed `(base, length)` views are exposed and
ownership never crosses the boundary.

## Options matrix

Scores are 0-5; 5 is best fit or lowest cost for the category. `COMPLEXITY` is
therefore a feasibility score, not a size score.

| Option | Safety | Determinism | Complexity | M1.35 fit | FFI | Native ABI | Self-host | Apps | Collections | Errors | Generic dependence | Runtime cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A. Move-only owned values with lexical borrows | 5 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 |
| B. Explicit integer-like handles and manual destroy | 2 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 4 |
| C. Reference-counted shared buffers | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 2 |
| D. Copy-on-write immutable text/buffers | 4 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 3 |

Option A is selected. Option B makes use-after-close and ownership transfer
caller obligations visible in every program. Option C introduces hidden
destruction and cycle policy. Option D makes mutation and FFI aliasing less
direct but still requires a hidden shared-owner policy. Option A gives S3 one
explicit owner, deterministic destruction, and a small future path to numeric
collections without requiring general generics.

## Decision quality record

### Public model

`DECISION=` ordinary move-only owned values with lexical borrows.

`ALTERNATIVES=`handles, reference counting, copy-on-write.

`WHY_SELECTED=`one owner and explicit clone preserve determinism and make the
future collection/resource rules composable.

`WHY_REJECTED=`handles weaken static safety; shared models add hidden lifetime
and destruction policy.

`CURRENT_S3_EVIDENCE=`frame-local ownership, explicit mutability, conservative
alias analysis, existing `&`/`&mut` reference syntax.

`EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE=`Rust Vec documents ownership/reallocation and Zig documents
that buffer slice lifetimes end at resize; S3 adopts the problem boundary, not
their full languages.

`FUTURE_IMPACT=`M1.40 can add closed numeric collections using the same owner
and borrow rules.

### Allocation

`DECISION=`single target-provided allocator, explicit constructors/reserve,
exact capacity, no automatic growth in M1.39.

`ALTERNATIVES=`implicit geometric growth, user-injected allocators, arena-only
storage.

`WHY_SELECTED=`exact reserve makes allocation and capacity deterministic while
avoiding a full allocator API.

`WHY_REJECTED=`implicit growth makes capacity/failure behavior less explicit;
injection and arenas are M1.43/M1.40-sized decisions.

`CURRENT_S3_EVIDENCE=`existing runtime limits and no public heap/allocator.

`EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE=`Rust Vec separates length/capacity and reallocation;
Zig's ArrayList documents invalidation of items after resize.

`FUTURE_IMPACT=`M1.40 may define a growth policy for collections without
changing bytes/text ownership.

### Aliasing and lifetime

`DECISION=`multiple shared or one mutable borrow; lexical end; no borrow across
owner move, drop, reserve, push, append, or return of a local owner.

`ALTERNATIVES=`runtime-only checks, explicit lifetime parameters, unrestricted
aliases.

`WHY_SELECTED=`static lexical rules fit existing references and reject dangling
storage without adding lifetime syntax.

`WHY_REJECTED=`runtime-only checks are too late; lifetime parameters are too
large; unrestricted aliases conflict with reallocation.

`CURRENT_S3_EVIDENCE=`existing `&`/`&mut` AST and conservative memory effects.

`EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE=`Rust slice lifetime/reallocation guidance and Zig resize
invalidation show the concrete hazard.

`FUTURE_IMPACT=`indexed element borrows remain a separate decision.
