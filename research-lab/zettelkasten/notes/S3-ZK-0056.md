# S3-ZK-0056 - Native recomputation can preserve a public trust boundary

## Claim

When a native consumer already owns the validated CFG and use/def facts needed
for a bounded safety predicate, fail-closed recomputation can be safer and
smaller than transporting a general proof through a public intermediate
representation.

## Evidence

P8.4 matched the P8.3 safe population exactly: 416 static sites and 1660
dynamic events. The production emitter consumed 402 sites and 1558 dynamic
events, removing only the corresponding native initialization-check pairs.
Across 24 Linux O0/O1 pairs, semantics matched baseline. Public/manual Assembly,
calls, references, slices, aliases, joins, loops, address-taken registers and
unknown facts retained the checked fallback.

## Consequence

The selected production model is `CONSERVATIVE_NATIVE_RECOMPUTATION`, not a
generic proof-transport framework. Public Assembly cannot claim a private safety
fact, while the native consumer still removes a measured redundant realization.
Structural counters improved, but loads/stores did not; runtime and compile-time
results remain unavailable under a comparable protocol.
