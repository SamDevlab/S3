# S3-ZK-0057 - Observed frame residency is not established spill causality

## Claim

An x86 value with no assigned physical register can be observed as stack
resident without proving that the traffic is a removable spill, that a
different allocation would preserve all observers, or that the frame family
is the dominant native bottleneck.

## Evidence

The P9 validated site-weighted model covered 15 workloads. On O1 it counted
143151 modelled native dynamic x86 executions. The broad frame class was
19725, scalar frame-value traffic was 7807, and stack-resident frame-value
traffic was 5431, exactly 3.793895956018% of the O1 model. The fixed-array
shape already used direct indexed addressing and had no separate array-base
reload family. Assembly, SSA and optimized opcode counts aligned in the
examined fixed-array, branch-heavy and JSMN cases.

## Consequence

The broad frame and fixed-array hypotheses must not be promoted from observed
residency alone. Register allocation is not automatically the next target.
The strongest remaining small experiment is a proof-bearing bounds and
validity analysis for loop-carried TLOAD/TSTORE sites, with failure order,
initialization, immutability, instruction-limit and checked-fallback controls.
